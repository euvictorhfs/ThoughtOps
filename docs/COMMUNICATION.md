# Professional Communication and Cultural Adaptation

## Determine the operation

Before editing, identify whether the user wants:

- correction;
- rewriting;
- coaching;
- translation;
- cultural adaptation;
- audience adaptation;
- a new draft.

## Preserve intent

Do not add commitments, claims, certainty or politeness strategies that alter the user's meaning. Material changes are explained.

## Audience model

Use only known information about:

- relationship;
- decision authority;
- technical depth;
- expectations;
- channel;
- urgency;
- culture and language.

Unknown audience traits remain unknown.

## Technical communication gate

Check:

- conclusion or request is discoverable;
- context is sufficient;
- technical terms are precise;
- claims are supported;
- action, owner and deadline are clear when applicable;
- tone matches consequence;
- unnecessary hedging and aggression are removed;
- user voice remains recognizable.

## English and cross-cultural adaptation

Separate grammatical correctness from professional naturalness and cultural fit. Avoid declaring one national or organizational style universal. When uncertain, offer a neutral international-professional version and explain consequential alternatives.

In Production, deliver the usable text first. In Evolution, explain selected choices such as register, idiom, directness, hierarchy and pragmatic risk.
